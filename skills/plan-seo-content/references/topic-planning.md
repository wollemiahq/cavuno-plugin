## Why topics, not keyword lists

The Cavuno posts make the case for fewer, stronger pages:

- The AEO guide cites Graphite's "AEO Topics" framework: find clusters of
  related questions and "create one complete page per cluster rather than
  thin pages for each question". One page can answer head, mid-tail and
  long-tail questions on the same topic.
- The content marketing guide: "Depth over breadth. 20 thorough pieces
  beat 100 thin ones", "12 excellent hiring guides beat 52 generic career
  tips posts", and Backlinko reached 100K monthly visitors "with just 35
  posts". It also says the search volume of every keyword mapping to a
  topic is that topic's traffic opportunity.
- Treat the blog as a library, not a publication: evergreen over timely,
  and updating what works over always publishing something new.

## Group variants into topics

| Same topic (one page) | Separate topics (separate pages) |
| --- | --- |
| Wording variants: "how to become a vet tech", "vet tech career path" | Different reader: "how to hire a vet tech" (employer) vs "how to become a vet tech" (seeker) |
| Questions the same reader asks about one subject: duties, skills, pay | Different format expected: a resume example vs an interview question list |
| A city or level that changes nothing in the answer | A city or level that changes the answer: "vet tech salary in Texas" with its own data |

Disaggregate a broad subject into specific angles that each serve one
reader (the content marketing guide's "ranch-style" matrix): for a vet
board, resume examples split into vet tech, receptionist and new graduate
resumes, because each reader needs a different answer.

## Map each topic to a page type

| Searchers expect | Right page | Blog post? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open roles: "[role] jobs", "[role] jobs in [city]", "remote [role] jobs" | Cavuno category, skill or location page | No |
| A specific employer's openings | Cavuno company page | No |
| A role's pay with current data | Salary guide built from the board's jobs, or a location page | Yes, if the board has enough salaried jobs to say something |
| How to get into or progress in a role | Career guide | Yes |
| Interview questions, resume or cover letter help | Guide or example post | Yes |
| Where to post jobs, how to hire a role, job description templates | Employer guide or template | Yes, the highest business value for most boards |
| A product, tool or forum thread dominates the results | Not an article | Usually no; say what would win |

The SEO guide orders keyword groups by business value: employer pain
points ("where to post [job type] jobs", "how to hire [title]", "[title]
job description template") first, then job seeker transactional searches
(served by board pages), then informational searches that build authority.

## Cannibalisation decisions

Run after the query-by-page Search Console read. For each query where two
or more posts earn impressions:

| What you find | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Both posts answer the same intent for the same reader | Merge into the stronger URL (more clicks, more links); plan it in `refresh-and-prune-content` |
| Same subject, different reader or format | Differentiate: sharpen titles, headings and intros; link each to the other |
| One post owns the query; the other mentions it in passing | Leave it; add a link from the passing mention to the owner |
| A post competes with a board page (category, location) | Point the post to the board page; drop the job listing angle from the post |
| A planned new post would share a topic with an existing post | Refresh or expand the existing post instead |

## Choose an action

| Situation | Action |
| --- | --- |
| No page covers the topic and an article fits the intent | New article |
| A post covers it but is thin, dated, or misses the current intent | Refresh |
| Two or more posts split the topic | Merge |
| A post covers the topic but lacks one subtopic | Expand a section |
| A board page should win the query | Improve the non-blog page (category description, company profile) |
| Wrong intent, no business value, or nothing new to add | Skip, and say why |

## Prioritize

Score each item on separate axes; don't fold them into one number:

- **Business value:** does the reader become a subscriber, applicant or
  paying employer? The content marketing guide cites Grow and Convert:
  bottom-of-funnel content converted at 4.78% versus 0.19% for
  top-of-funnel awareness content.
- **Search viability:** first-party Search Console signals first (the
  board already appears for the query), then the user's tool estimates.
- **Information gain:** what only this board can add. Its own job data
  (salary ranges, who is hiring, demand by category or location), operator
  experience, an employer or expert contributor, a template. "Longer" or
  "more comprehensive" is not information gain.
- **Effort and dependencies:** data to gather, an author to recruit, a
  merge that must happen first.

Sequence: start where the board's topical authority is strongest (its core
roles and skills) before broader career advice. A new board with no
candidate audience can start with seeker content tied to job discovery; a
board with an existing audience (a newsletter, members) can lead with
employer content.

## Cadence and balance

The content marketing guide's publishing table, by resources:

| Resources | New content | Updates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solo operator, 5-10 hours a week | 1 a week | 1-2 a month |
| Part-time content person | 2 a week | 4-6 a month |
| Full-time hire or agency | 3-5 a week | 8-10 a month |

It suggests roughly 80% evergreen library content and 20% timely content,
and 20-30% of content time on updates. With time for one post a month,
make it a bottom-of-funnel piece.
